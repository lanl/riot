Material Strength

The strength package augments the hydrodynamics of Chapter Hydrodynamics with an elastic–plastic constitutive response for solid materials. When enabled, materials support a deviatoric stress \(\mathsf{s}\) that modifies the momentum and energy fluxes, evolves in time with the flow, and is limited by a yield condition. Strength is applied per material: a material must be flagged as strong and assigned a strength model.

Governing Equations

Strength in RIOT is a per-material property: each strong material \(m\) carries its own deviatoric stress \(\mathsf{s}_m\), shear modulus \(G_m\), and yield strength \(Y_m\), and evolves them independently. The hydrodynamics, however, sees a single bulk deviatoric stress \(\mathsf{s}\) aggregated from the per-material stresses (Section Per-Material and Bulk Quantities). This section follows that per-material \(\to\) bulk path.

The full bulk Cauchy stress is decomposed into an isotropic (pressure) part and a traceless deviatoric part,

\[\mathsf{\sigma} = -p\,\mathsf{I} + \mathsf{s}, \qquad \mathrm{tr}\,\mathsf{s} = 0 ,\]

where \(p\) comes from the PTE closure (Chapter Hydrodynamics) and the bulk deviatoric stress \(\mathsf{s}\) enters the bulk momentum and energy fluxes through the terms \(-\mathsf{s}\) and \(-\mathsf{s}\!\cdot\!\vec{v}\) (see the hydro equations in Chapter Hydrodynamics).

Per-Material Hypoelastic Stress Evolution

For each strong material \(m\), the conserved per-material deviatoric stress \(\bar\rho_m\mathsf{s}_m\) is advected with the flow and sourced by a hypoelastic constitutive law using the Jaumann (corotational, objective) stress rate, which keeps the response frame-invariant under rigid rotation:

\[\frac{\partial \left(\bar\rho_m\mathsf{s}_m\right)}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\bar\rho_m\mathsf{s}_m\,\vec{v}\right) \;=\; 2\bar\rho_m G_m\,\mathsf{e} + \bar\rho_m\!\left(\mathsf{w}\!\cdot\!\mathsf{s}_m - \mathsf{s}_m\!\cdot\!\mathsf{w}\right).\]

Here \(G_m\) is the material’s shear modulus and \(\bar\rho_m\) its cell-volume-averaged density, both per material. The strain-rate tensor \(\mathsf{e}\) and spin tensor \(\mathsf{w}\), by contrast, are bulk quantities: they are computed once per cell from the single velocity field common to all materials in the cell. Thus every material in a cell is driven by the same velocity gradient but evolves its own stress with its own shear modulus. The strain-rate and spin tensors are the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of that velocity gradient,

\[\begin{split}e_{ij} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\partial_j v_i + \partial_i v_j\right) - \tfrac{1}{3}\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec{v})\,\delta_{ij}, \\ w_{ij} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\partial_j v_i - \partial_i v_j\right).\end{split}\]

Because \(\mathsf{s}_m\) is symmetric and traceless, only five of its components are independent and stored (\(s_{xx}, s_{xy}, s_{xz}, s_{yy}, s_{yz}\)); the sixth follows from \(s_{zz} = -s_{xx} - s_{yy}\).

Per-Material Yield and Radial Return

Each material’s elastic stress is limited by its own von Mises yield criterion. Defining the second invariant of the per-material deviatoric stress,

\[J_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{s}_m\!:\!\mathsf{s}_m = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(s_{xx}^2 + s_{yy}^2 + s_{zz}^2\right) + s_{xy}^2 + s_{xz}^2 + s_{yz}^2 ,\]

material \(m\) yields when \(\mathsf{s}_m\!:\!\mathsf{s}_m\) exceeds \(\tfrac{2}{3}Y_m^2\), where \(Y_m\) is its yield strength. When the trial stress lies outside the yield surface, a radial-return correction scales it back onto the surface,

\[\mathsf{s}_m \leftarrow \sqrt{\frac{2Y_m^2}{3\,\mathsf{s}_m\!:\!\mathsf{s}_m}}\;\mathsf{s}_m ,\]

and the material’s accumulated (equivalent) plastic strain \(\varepsilon_{p,m}\) is incremented accordingly. The radial return and failure ramp are applied per material. The plastic work released by each material during its return is summed over materials and deposited into the bulk total energy, so plastic dissipation heats the cell.

Failure

As a material’s density falls toward its failure threshold \(\rho_{\text{fail},m}\), its deviatoric stress is ramped smoothly to zero,

\[\mathsf{s}_m \leftarrow \min\!\left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_{\text{fail},m}},\,1\right)\mathsf{s}_m ,\]

so that a fully failed (e.g. spalled or rarefied) material carries no strength and reverts to hydrodynamic behavior.

Aggregation to the Bulk Stress

The hydrodynamic fluxes use a bulk deviatoric stress and bulk shear modulus formed as volume-fraction-weighted sums of the per-material values (Section Per-Material and Bulk Quantities),

\[\mathsf{s} = \sum_m f_m\,\mathsf{s}_m, \qquad G = \sum_m f_m\,G_m .\]

In practice the per-material stresses are reconstructed to cell faces and then combined with these \(f_m\) weights, and the resulting bulk stress is passed to the strength-aware Riemann solver. This is the sole channel by which per-material strength influences the shared momentum and energy equations.

Strength Models

Each strong material is assigned a strength model that supplies the shear modulus \(G\) and yield strength \(Y\). The available model is listed in the table below.

Strength models (modelname).

Option

Name

Description

epp

Elastic–perfectly-plastic

Constant shear modulus \(G_0\) and constant yield strength \(Y_0\).

A pressure- and temperature-dependent Steinberg–Guinan model (Steinberg, Cochran & Guinan, J. Appl. Phys. 51, 1498, 1980) is present in the source but not yet enabled.

Example

An elastic–perfectly-plastic beryllium, flagged as strong and pointed at a strength-model block:

riot.input("physics", strength=True)
riot.input("material0", strong=True,
           strength_model="beryllium_strength")
riot.input("beryllium_strength", modelname="epp",
           G0=1.519e12,   # shear modulus
           Y0=3.30e9,     # yield strength
           rho_fail=0.5)  # failure density
