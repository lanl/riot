Magnetohydrodynamics

Setting mhd = true in the <physics> block extends the hydro solver of Chapter Hydrodynamics to ideal MHD. The magnetic field \(\vec{B}\) is in Heaviside–Lorentz units, so the magnetic pressure is \(B^2/2\), with no factors of \(4\pi\) or \(\mu_0\). A field \(\vec{B}_G\) given in Gaussian units enters as \(\vec{B} = \vec{B}_G/\sqrt{4\pi}\). With the magnetic terms the momentum and energy equations become

\[\begin{split}\frac{\partial \left(\rho\vec{v}\right)}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot\!\left[\rho\vec{v}\otimes\vec{v} + \left(p + \tfrac{1}{2}B^2\right)\mathsf{I} - \vec{B}\otimes\vec{B} - \mathsf{s}\right] &= \vec{0}, \\[2pt] \frac{\partial E}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot\!\left[\left(E + p + \tfrac{1}{2}B^2\right)\vec{v} - \left(\vec{v}\!\cdot\!\vec{B}\right)\vec{B} - \mathsf{s}\!\cdot\!\vec{v}\right] &= 0, \\[2pt] \frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\times\!\vec{\mathcal{E}} &= \vec{0}, \qquad \vec{\mathcal{E}} = -\vec{v}\times\vec{B},\end{split}\]

where the total energy now includes the magnetic energy, \(E = u + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho|\vec{v}|^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}B^2\). The bulk internal energy passed to the PTE closure is recovered by subtracting both the kinetic and the magnetic energy. The material densities, volume fractions and the PTE closure are handled exactly as in the hydro case.

Constrained Transport

The evolved field is face-centered: ffbulk::bfield stores the component normal to each cell face. It is updated with constrained transport: the EMF \(\vec{\mathcal{E}}\) lives on cell edges, and each face is updated by the circulation of \(\vec{\mathcal{E}}\) around its boundary (Stokes’ theorem). This keeps the discrete divergence of \(\vec{B}\) at round-off for all time. The edge EMFs are built from the face EMFs returned by the Riemann solver with the upwind corner integration of Gardiner & Stone (2005). The upwind direction at each face is chosen from the mass flux with the continuous weight of Athena++. The EMFs are stored as the edge fluxes of ffbulk::bfield, so Parthenon’s flux correction keeps them consistent across fine/coarse boundaries.

Riemann Solver and Reconstruction

MHD always uses the HLLD solver. Any other riemann choice is replaced by hlld with a warning, and hlld without mhd is an error. The cell-centered field ccbulk::bfield (the average of the face fields) is reconstructed with the other bulk primitives, using the same recon and plm_slope_limit. The field component normal to a face is not reconstructed: both sides of the Riemann problem use the face value from ffbulk::bfield. The corner integration needs face EMFs one cell beyond the block interior in each transverse direction, so MHD needs one more ghost cell than the same reconstruction in pure hydro. The time step uses the fast magnetosonic speed in each direction.

Geometry and Refinement

MHD runs in UniformCartesian and UniformCylindrical coordinates. The CT update uses the true edge lengths and face areas, and in cylindrical coordinates the cell-centered field is interpolated to the cell centroid. The magnetic contributions to the geometric source terms (the \(r\)-momentum term \(\tfrac{1}{2}(B_r^2 + B_z^2 - B_\phi^2)/r\) and the corresponding \(B_r B_\phi\) term in the \(\phi\)-momentum) are included. With static or adaptive refinement, new fine faces are filled with the divergence-preserving prolongation of Tóth & Roe (2002), and coarse faces are restricted by area averaging. MHD cannot be combined with material strength, ionization, radiation transport or the BHR mix model. Sparse physics is turned off automatically when MHD is enabled.

Initial Conditions

The field is set from one of two optional methods of the Python class named in the <regions> block (Chapter The Python Interface). Both fill an \((N,3)\) output array in place:

  • f_b_vector_potential(self, pos, a) returns the vector potential \(\vec{A}\) at the cell edge centers. ffbulk::bfield is set to its discrete curl, so the initial \(\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec{B}\) is zero to round-off. This is the preferred method.

  • f_b_bfield(self, pos, b) returns \(\vec{B}\) at the face centers; each face keeps its normal component. Initialization stops with an error unless the resulting discrete \(\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec{B}\) vanishes to round-off. This suits fields that are uniform, or vary only along the face directions.

Without either method \(\vec{B} = 0\). Positions and vector components follow the same Cartesian convention as the other state setters: in cylindrical coordinates self.x, self.y and self.z index the \(r\), \(\phi\) and \(z\) components. The magnetic energy of the initial field is added to the total energy automatically.

Diagnostics

The history file gains the total magnetic energy ME and the maximum of \(|\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec{B}|\,\Delta x\) (max_divB_dx, with \(\Delta x\) the smallest width of each cell), which should stay at round-off. The cell-centered field ccbulk::bfield and the divergence ccbulk::divb can be added to the HDF5 outputs.

Example Decks

inputs/mhd/ holds the Brio–Wu shock tube (brio_wu.py), the Orszag–Tang vortex (orszag_tang.py), field loop advection in 2D and 3D (field_loop.py, --3d for the loop inclined to the grid of Gardiner & Stone 2008), and two cylindrical problems: field loop advection in \(r\)–\(z\) (field_loop_rz.py) and a rotating magnetized pinch equilibrium (rotating_pinch.py). Each deck takes --help.
